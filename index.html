6-Week Program · 4 Days/Week · 15-20 Min

Posture Reset: Pelvis + Upper Back

One program for the whole chain: anterior pelvic tilt, rounded upper back, and forward head. The pattern is the same top to bottom, deep stabilizers asleep (transverse abdominis, deep neck flexors, lower traps), surface muscles overworking (lower back, hip flexors, pecs, upper traps). So every session strengthens the deep layer, releases the tight layer, and practices the stacked position. No front planks, per your massage therapist.

Daily habits (these do half the work)

  • Couch stretch 60s per side on rest days too, ideally after sitting.
  • Full-body posture cue 3x a day: exhale fully, ribs drop down, pelvis tucks about 10%, then glide the head straight back (double-chin, not looking down) and let the shoulders hang. Stack, don't strain.
  • Hourly desk reset: 5 chin tucks + 5 slow shoulder rolls back. Takes 20 seconds.
  • Raise your screen to eye level and bring your phone up instead of your head down. This single change beats any exercise for forward head.
  • When lifting heavy, brace with ribs stacked over pelvis, not flared up, and keep a neutral neck (look at the floor a few meters ahead on deadlifts, not the mirror).
  • Sleep: one medium pillow, not a stack. Back sleepers can add a pillow under the knees for the lower back.

Lifting alongside this program

Prioritize · pulls (aim for 2 pull sets per 1 press set)

  • Chest-supported row / seal row · upper back work with zero lower back load. Squeeze blades down and back, chin tucked.
  • Lat pulldown / pull-up · ribs down, slight tuck, no arching to grind reps. Hollow-ish body on pull-ups.
  • Single-arm cable row · resist rotation as you row, it doubles as core work.

Prioritize · legs & hips

  • Front squat / goblet squat · the front load forces ribs down and an upright torso. A loaded posture drill.
  • Hip thrust · glutes at full power with a posterior tilt at lockout, no back arch.
  • RDL · strengthens hamstrings + glutes, the muscles that pull your pelvis back to neutral. Neutral spine, don't arch hard at the top.
  • Split squat / RFE split squat · the rear leg gets a loaded hip flexor stretch. Torso tall, pelvis tucked.
  • Seated or lying hamstring curl · hamstrings are posterior tilters; strengthen them shortened so they do their job without strain.

Do consciously, not banned

These invite a lower back arch. Keep them only with a solid ribs-stacked brace.

  • Low-bar back squat · consider front or high-bar squats for these 6 weeks.
  • Standing overhead press · easy to hyperextend under. A landmine press gives the same pattern without the arch.
  • Heavy deadlifts · fine with a good brace, but do them earlier in the week than Days 1 and 4 so a fried core doesn't ruin your dead bug quality.
  • Pressing volume overall · keep bench/press moderate for now and match every press with a pull. Short pecs are half your upper-back problem.

Optional: Back + Biceps session (pairs with Day 3)

Do Day 3's face pulls and band pull-aparts first as your warmup, then this, then finish with Day 3's pallof presses, side planks, and reverse crunches. Ribs down and a slight tuck on every rep. Skip barbell bent-over rows and deadlifts on this day, the goal is upper back volume without erector overtime. ~60 min total.

  • Lat pulldown or pull-ups · 4 × 8-10 · no arching to grind reps, hollow-ish body.
  • Chest-supported row · 4 × 10 · incline bench + dumbbells or machine, squeeze blades down and back, chin tucked.
  • Single-arm cable row · 3 × 10/side · resist the rotation, it's a bonus pallof.
  • Straight-arm pulldown · 3 × 12 · lats with zero lower back load, ribs stacked.
  • EZ-bar or cable curl · 3 × 10
  • Incline dumbbell curl · 3 × 10 · the bench takes your lower back out of it.
  • Hammer curl · 2 × 12

Optional: Back + Biceps + Core, Session B

Alternate with the Back + Biceps session above week to week, or run both if you train back twice, they share almost no exercises. Warm up with foam roller t-spine extensions 2 × 8 and band pull-aparts 2 × 15. Chin tucked and ribs down on every rep. ~60-70 min total.

  • Neutral-grip pull-ups or close-grip lat pulldown · 4 × 8-10 · the neutral grip makes it easier to keep ribs stacked.
  • Seated cable row · 3 × 10-12 · chest tall, squeeze blades down and back, no leaning back to yank.
  • Half-kneeling single-arm lat pulldown · 3 × 10/side · kneel like your landmine press, rear glute squeezed. Lat + anti-tilt core in one.
  • Prone incline Y-raise · 3 × 10, light · lower traps, the muscle that literally un-rounds you.
  • Spider curl · 3 × 10 · chest on incline bench, fully back-safe.
  • Bayesian cable curl · 3 × 10 · big stretch; ribs down, don't let the cable arch you backwards.
  • Reverse EZ-bar curl · 2 × 12 · forearms and elbow health.
  • Hanging knee raise w/ posterior tilt · 3 × 8-10 · curl the tailbone under at the top; reverse crunch if grip is fried.
  • Weighted dead bug · 3 × 8/side · light plate pressed to the ceiling, back flat.
  • Suitcase march · 3 × 30s/side · one heavy dumbbell, march in place slowly, dead-level hips.

Optional: Chest + Shoulders session (pairs with Day 1)

Do Day 1's 90/90 breathing first, it grooves the ribs-down brace you'll press from. One rule for the whole session: ribs stacked, no powerlifter arch, exhale on every push. Finish with the doorway pec stretch 40s/side, pressing shortens the pecs, so pay the toll on the way out. Keep pressing to ~2 days a week and hold the 2:1 pull-to-press ratio. ~60 min total.

  • Flat dumbbell or machine chest press · 4 × 8-10 · feet planted, lower back keeps light contact with the bench, no arching to press more.
  • Incline dumbbell press (~30°) · 3 × 10 · upper chest, and the incline makes arching harder. Your safest heavy press.
  • Cable fly or pec deck · 3 × 12 · pause 1s in the deep stretch each rep, it doubles as pec mobility work.
  • Seated dumbbell shoulder press (back on pad) · 3 × 8-10 · the pad polices your arch. Landmine press 3 × 8/side is the most tilt-friendly alternative.
  • Lateral raises · 3 × 12-15 · totally back-neutral, build all the side delt you want.
  • Reverse fly or band pull-apart · 2 × 15 · small pull finisher to balance the pushing.
  • Skip on this day · standing barbell overhead press (invites hyperextension) and deep dips (crank the shoulder and arch the back).

Optional: Legs + Core session

Hamstring and glute biased, the muscles that pull your pelvis back to neutral. Warm up with couch stretch 45s/side and glute bridges w/ posterior tilt 2 × 10. Ribs stacked and exhale-brace on every heavy rep. If you run this on a Day 2 day, do legs first and Day 2's stretching after as the cooldown. ~70 min total.

  • Front squat or goblet squat · 4 × 6-10 · the front load forces the upright, ribs-down torso.
  • Romanian deadlift · 3 × 8-10 · hips back, neutral spine, finish by squeezing glutes, not by arching.
  • Rear-foot-elevated split squat · 3 × 8/side · pelvis tucked, torso tall, rear leg gets a loaded hip flexor stretch.
  • Hip thrust · 3 × 8-10 · posterior tilt at lockout, chin tucked, no arch to finish reps.
  • Seated or lying hamstring curl · 3 × 10-12 · hamstrings shortened and strong, exactly what an APT body needs.
  • Standing calf raise · 3 × 12-15 · optional but free.
  • Hanging knee raise w/ posterior tilt or reverse crunch · 3 × 10 · core block starts here.
  • Copenhagen plank or side plank · 3 × 20-30s/side
  • Heavy suitcase carry · 3 × 40s/side · stay stacked under load.
  • Skip on leg day · back extensions and GHD hypers (erectors are already overworked), grinding low-bar squats, and leg press depth past where your butt rolls off the pad.

Skip for now

  • Front planks (per your therapist, they feed your lower back tension pattern).
  • Weighted back extensions, supermans, and heavy direct lower-back work. Your erectors are already overworked.
  • Forcing "shoulders back, chest up" military posture. It just arches your lower back more and swaps one tension for another. Ribs down first, then the shoulders hang in place naturally.
  • Sit-ups and leg raises done with an arched lower back. If your back arches off the floor, regress the exercise.
  • Extra pressing volume. Your pecs are already short and pulling you forward, so keep bench/press work moderate for these 6 weeks and match every press with a pull.
If any exercise causes sharp pain (not muscle burn), or tingling/numbness with the neck work, skip it and tell me. I'm not a medical professional, and since you have real lower back tension, a physio check is worth it if things persist or worsen.
